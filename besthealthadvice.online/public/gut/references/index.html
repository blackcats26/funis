References

Last updated: November 10, 2026

The studies below evaluated the types of ingredients found in Gut Active (psyllium husk, oat bran, apple pectin, glucomannan, plum fruit and Lactobacillus acidophilus), often at amounts different from those in the product. They did not evaluate Gut Active itself.

Peer-reviewed studies on the types of ingredients in Gut Active

  • Yang J, Wang HP, Zhou L, Xu CF. Effect of dietary fiber on constipation: a meta analysis. World J Gastroenterol. 2012;18(48):7378-7383.
    Dietary fiber intake increased stool frequency in people with constipation.
  • McRorie JW, Daggy BP, Morel JG, et al. Psyllium is superior to docusate sodium for treatment of chronic constipation. Aliment Pharmacol Ther. 1998;12(5):491-497.
    Psyllium husk (an ingredient in Gut Active) increased stool water content and bowel movement frequency.
  • Attaluri A, Donahoe R, Valestin J, Brown K, Rao SSC. Randomised clinical trial: dried plums (prunes) vs. psyllium for constipation. Aliment Pharmacol Ther. 2011;33(7):822-828.
    Dried plums (plum fruit is an ingredient in Gut Active) improved spontaneous bowel movements and stool consistency.
  • Chen HL, Cheng HC, Wu WT, Liu YJ, Liu SY. Supplementation of konjac glucomannan into a low-fiber Chinese diet promoted bowel movement and improved colonic ecology in constipated adults. J Am Coll Nutr. 2008;27(1):102-108.
    Glucomannan (an ingredient in Gut Active) increased bowel movement frequency in constipated adults.
  • Dimidi E, Christodoulides S, Fragkos KC, Scott SM, Whelan K. The effect of probiotics on functional constipation in adults: a systematic review and meta-analysis of randomized controlled trials. Am J Clin Nutr. 2014;100(4):1075-1084.
    Probiotics as a group improved whole-gut transit time, stool frequency and consistency; effects varied by strain.
